Cognitive load theory

Activity

Test the cognitive load theory on yourself with this activity. Try to remember the steps without writing them down or looking at the map!

Tip: Make sure your sound is on, then select the 'Ask for directions' button to start.

Reflect

How did you find the experience of locating the cafe? Was it straightforward, or did you encounter moments of confusion? Did you need to repeat the activity, perhaps re-listening to the instructions or retracing your steps?

This highlights a fundamental aspect of cognitive load. When faced with an unfamiliar task, particularly one with multiple steps or abstract concepts, our working memory is placed under considerable strain. The need to hold and process numerous pieces of information simultaneously can quickly lead to overload. If you found yourself needing to repeat the activity, it likely indicates that your working memory was temporarily overwhelmed.

Expert voice

When introducing children to new concepts or activities, it's essential to recognise the limitations of their working memory. Complex, unfamiliar tasks can easily overwhelm them, hindering their ability to learn and retain information. By carefully considering the cognitive load of learning activities, we can create more effective and supportive learning environments that promote deeper understanding and retention.
